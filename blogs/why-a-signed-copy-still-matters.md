Books move through the world in many forms.

They arrive as paperbacks passed from one reader to another, ebooks opened late at night, library copies carrying the marks of many hands, gifts wrapped for a birthday, and books bought because someone needed a story at the right moment. Each form makes the same text available. Each has its own kind of value.

At JSM Cooperative, we also offer a signed copy of *The Man in the Ball Cap*. It is still the same mystery: the same A Coruña setting, the same Pepe Miguel case, the same questions, danger, symbols, and secrets waiting inside the story.

What changes is the path the book takes to reach you.

A signed copy is a direct connection. It is a physical book sent from the cooperative to a reader who has chosen to make the reading experience a little more personal.

## The Book as an Object

It is easy to talk about books only as containers for stories. The story matters most, of course. Without it, there is nothing to enter, remember, question, or share.

But books are also objects that travel through a life.

They sit on a shelf. They wait on a table. They get carried in a bag. They become gifts. They receive notes, bookmarks, folded corners, and the small evidence of a reader returning to them. A physical book can gather time around itself in a way that is difficult to measure and easy to recognize.

That is part of the appeal of a signed copy. The signature does not change the plot or make one reader more important than another. It simply marks a moment of connection between the book, its project, and the person who chose it.

For some readers, that connection matters because they collect books. For others, it matters because they are giving a story to someone else. For others, it is a way of choosing an edition that feels closer to the work behind the book.

The reasons can be quiet. They do not have to be grand to be real.

## A More Personal Route to the Reader

The signed edition of *The Man in the Ball Cap* is a paperback ordered directly through JSM Cooperative. It is priced at **$15 plus shipping**, personally signed, and includes the **#JoinTheCamino** inscription. During PayPal checkout, readers can also leave an optional inscription request if they would like the copy made out to someone.

That option makes the signed edition especially meaningful as a gift. A name inside a book can turn a purchase into a gesture. It can mark a birthday, a thank-you, a friendship, a reading group, or simply the decision to place a story in someone else's hands.

The personal element is not meant to compete with the book itself. It supports the experience of receiving it. The mystery still has to do its own work: introduce Pepe Miguel, draw the reader into A Coruña, and make the next clue difficult to ignore.

But the copy has a trail back to the project. It has passed through a more human route before it arrives at your door.

## The Camino Inside the Inscription

The #JoinTheCamino inscription is a small phrase with a larger meaning inside JSM Cooperative.

The Camino is the shared path that connects books, readers, newsletters, donations, creative work, nonprofit-aligned impact, and the community around them. It is not a demand that every reader participate in every part of the mission. It is an open invitation to see that stories can travel beyond the page.

When someone chooses a signed copy, they are supporting the same idea in a direct way. The book becomes part of a larger experiment: can creative work gather people, create conversation, and help sustain work with purpose?

JSM Cooperative believes it can.

That belief does not ask the reader to overlook the simple pleasure of a good mystery. It starts there. A reader should choose *The Man in the Ball Cap* because they want a story with a private investigator, a city shaped by Atlantic weather and memory, and a case that becomes stranger as it unfolds.

The Camino is what surrounds that reading experience. It is the larger path the book helps make possible.

## A Mystery Rooted in A Coruña

For readers arriving here from the A Coruña Through the Lens series, the signed copy is also a way to carry a little of that atmosphere into the reading experience.

The city in the novel is not a postcard. Existing JSM material describes *The Man in the Ball Cap* as a mystery rooted in A Coruña and Galicia, where Pepe Miguel follows a case through hidden motives, symbols, occult rituals, memory, and danger. The setting matters because it gives the story texture: stone, weather, coastline, distance, and the feeling that a familiar place can hold more than it first reveals.

The signed edition does not add a new chapter or change the mystery. It simply gives readers who feel drawn to that world a direct way to bring the book home.

You can [read a free preview](/book#preview) first. A mystery should earn its reader. The opening pages are there to let you decide whether the voice, pace, and atmosphere are for you.

## Why Direct Support Matters

JSM Cooperative is built around a straightforward conviction: creative work can become part of community-powered impact. Book purchases help support the cooperative's nonprofit work, and the current commitment is clear: **100% of novel profits donated**.

That does not mean a signed copy needs to carry the whole mission by itself. No one book can do that. But each reader who buys, shares, reviews, recommends, or gives the novel helps the work move forward.

Support becomes visible in small actions:

- choosing a book for yourself or someone else
- sharing a page that made you curious
- recommending the mystery to a reader who enjoys place-driven suspense
- joining the Camino newsletter for future updates
- giving directly when the mission speaks to you

These are not interchangeable actions, and no one is required to take them all. They are simply different ways a community begins to form around a story.

## Choose the Edition That Fits You

There is no wrong route into *The Man in the Ball Cap*.

You can read the preview before deciding. You can choose a retailer edition. You can choose the Spanish edition. You can join the Camino subscription. Or you can order a signed direct copy because the personal connection matters to you.

The important thing is that the story reaches readers.

For those who want a paperback with a signature, a #JoinTheCamino inscription, and an optional personal note, the signed edition is there. It is a book made to be read, kept, shared, and remembered.
